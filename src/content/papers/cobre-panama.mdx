I am running an event study of how the stock market priced the shutdown of First Quantum's Cobre Panamá copper mine. I compare First Quantum's abnormal returns around the closure with those of other copper producers, using a market model. ## The question

[TODO: one or two sentences on what happened to the mine and when]

The closure can show up in two places. For First Quantum it is a loss of future cash flow, and the question is how much of that loss the market priced on the day and how much it had already priced in. For other copper producers the sign is not obvious: less supply could raise the copper price and help them, while a shutdown ordered by a government could make investors worry about country risk at mines elsewhere.

## What could be wrong with it

The event date may not be one day. If the news came out over several days, or leaked before the announcement, a short event window will miss part of the price move, and a long one will pick up unrelated news.

Other news on the same days can contaminate the result. Copper prices and mining stocks move together, so a shock to the whole sector on the event date would look like a reaction to the closure.

The comparison producers share one event date. Their abnormal returns are correlated with each other on that day, so treating them as independent would make the standard errors too small.
