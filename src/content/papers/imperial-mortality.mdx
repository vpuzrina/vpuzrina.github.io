Since September 2026 I have been a research assistant to Artem Vyshinskiy, a PhD researcher in economics at Boston University, on a project about the 1892–93 cholera epidemic in Imperial Russia, funded by a BU research grant. I digitize monthly deaths by uezd (district) from scanned Russian-language statistical yearbooks: 16 provinces, 72 months each, from 1888 to 1893, plus medical-capacity data for 1892. Before a table goes into the panel, I check every row and column against the totals printed in the source. The panel is merged with an MIT famine dataset and used for regressions in R and Stata.

## The checks

A transcribed table passes only when every row and every column adds up to the total printed in the yearbook. When one row and one column both miss by the same amount, the likely error is the cell where they cross, often two swapped digits. When a row misses but every column adds up, the cells are right and the printed total is wrong. That is an error in the source, and I correct it. ## What could be wrong with it

Sum checks catch any single error that changes a total. They miss two errors in the same row and column that cancel out, and they miss a figure that was already wrong when the yearbook's compilers added it into the printed total.

The crosswalk is the second weak point. If a district appears under a different name or with different borders in the two sources, it can be paired with the wrong one.

The third is registration. The yearbooks count registered deaths. During an epidemic, registration may lag or break down, so monthly counts for 1892 could understate deaths in the districts hit hardest.
